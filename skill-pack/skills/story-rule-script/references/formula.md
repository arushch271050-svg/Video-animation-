# The formula

## Numbers
- Length 65–72 s, 14–22 sentences, mostly 4–10 words each. Fast, flat, calm narrator; pause only before the twist.
- Second person, present tense: "you" do everything. Never "I".

## Line 1 — the hook rule
`If you <odd but concrete situation + a number/time>, don't <what everyone would do by instinct>.`
- The rule runs AGAINST instinct (the viewer must think "why?").
- A concrete number or time in the line: 2 a.m., 700 km, 4 %, 35,000 ft, the 12th tape.
Examples (write your own): If someone knocks at 2 a.m., don't answer. · If the fog covers the road, don't use
high beams. · If you have 18,000 to last the month, don't pay the rent first. · If the lift stops at a floor nobody pressed, don't step out.

## Line 2 — the second hook
Raise the stakes or hint at a hidden layer: "Because …", "But what's scarier than X is Y", "But X hides one thing".
In a continuing series line 2 restates the last cliffhanger as the reason.

## Skeleton
1. Hook rule + second hook.
2. A real fact with a number that explains the rule → used at once as an action.
3. Time + place stamp ("2:10 a.m. Room 604").
4. The sidekick animal notices first (ears up, stares at a gap, refuses to go on) — a clue for the viewer.
5. The plan, step by step: each step = one fact or observation + one action. Timestamps keep moving.
6. "You got everything right, except one thing."
7. The twist: ONE small physical detail that was visible earlier (rewatch → you can find it).
8. Cliffhanger, no explanation ("…and the rope is moving"). Optional choice card. At most one short aphorism, only as the last line.

## World and cast
- Real, local places and small real casts (a neighbour, an aunt, a friend with a short nickname) — not superheroes.
- A sidekick animal with a local name in most episodes: senses danger first, emotional anchor, never wasted.
- Decide early who can never die (the sidekick is a good candidate) and say it in the bible.

## Other topics (money / love / life)
Same skeleton. The "threat" is a life problem; the twist is emotional and turns back on the viewer
("the person who never asks to be paid back is you, at 60"). Lecture-style explainers and preachy endings
perform far worse than a story with a concrete twist.

## Voice
Short sentences, few conjunctions. Sensory and physical (sound, smell, temperature, light, distance).
Numbers often. No opinions while telling the story.

## Checklist
- [ ] Line 1 = "If you … (number) …, don't …", against instinct
- [ ] Line 2 raises the stakes / hides a layer (or restates last cliffhanger)
- [ ] Every fact has a number, is true, and becomes an action immediately
- [ ] Timestamps / countdown through the episode
- [ ] Sidekick notices before the people
- [ ] Twist = a physical detail planted earlier and visible on screen
- [ ] Ends on a cliffhanger (or a choice); no lecture
- [ ] 65–72 s; phrases ≤ 7 words, separated by spaces; numbers as words in the VO; no ranges
- [ ] Original story — only the mechanism is borrowed from any reference
